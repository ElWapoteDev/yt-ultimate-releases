# yt-geo

The zone a device position belongs to: the main city of the area and its country, named in Spanish the way concert
listings print them («Ciudad de México», «Nueva York», «México»). The application turns the position the operating
system reports into a zone with this crate, so the position never leaves the device and is never stored. The crate is
a pure engine: no file, network, database or clock access, and equal positions always give equal zones. Its only
dependencies are `yt-core` (the bounds of `RegionPreferences`) and `thiserror`.

## API

- `Coordinates::new(latitude, longitude) -> Result<Coordinates, GeoError>`: decimal degrees (WGS 84). Values that
  are not finite, latitudes outside −90…90 and longitudes outside −180…180 are `GeoError::InvalidCoordinates`.
  `latitude()`, `longitude()` and `distance_km(other)` (great circle on a sphere of the IUGG mean radius, within 0.5 %
  of the ellipsoidal distance).
- `zone_at(Coordinates) -> Option<Zone>`: the zone around a position, `None` when no place lies within
  `MAX_PLACE_DISTANCE_KM` (200 km: open sea, polar regions).
- `Zone { city, country }`: `&'static str` names from the embedded table; both fit a `yt_core::RegionPreferences`
  (at most `RegionPreferences::MAX_CHARS` characters, no control characters).
- Constants: `ZONE_RADIUS_KM` (30), `MAX_PLACE_DISTANCE_KM` (200), `SEAT_WEIGHT` (2).

## Algorithm

1. The nearest place of the table is found by a linear scan. Its country is the zone's country, so a city across a
   border is never chosen (Tijuana and San Diego, Ciudad Juárez and El Paso keep their own zones).
2. From that place, the zone moves to the place of the same country with the largest weight within `ZONE_RADIUS_KM`
   of it, and again from there, for as long as one outweighs the current place. The weight is the population, counted
   `SEAT_WEIGHT` times for seats of a country or of a first-level division (GeoNames `PPLC`, `PPLA`). Suburbs lead to
   the city their concerts are announced under: Ecatepec, Chalco and Cuautitlán Izcalli to Ciudad de México, San Pedro
   Garza García and Guadalupe to Monterrey, Zapopan and Tlajomulco to Guadalajara, Brooklyn to Nueva York, Getafe to
   Madrid. The weight grows at every step, so the walk always ends.

The seat weight exists because GeoNames counts metropolitan municipalities on their own and one can outnumber the core
city it belongs to (Zapopan, 1.48 million, beside Guadalajara, 1.39 million). Concerts are announced under the core
city, which is the seat.

## Data

`data/places.tsv` and `data/countries.tsv` are generated by `cargo run --locked -p xtask -- places` from the GeoNames
export dump (`cities15000`, `countryInfo.txt` and `alternateNamesV2`, downloaded into `target/geonames`; delete that
folder to rebuild from a newer dump). The current files come from the dump of 2026-11-10: 31,799 places of more than
15,000 people and capitals (1.1 MB) and 252 countries.

- Sections of a city and historical, abandoned or destroyed places (`PPLX`, `PPLH`, `PPLQ`, `PPLW`, `PPLCH`) are left
  out.
- A city keeps its preferred Spanish name, else the Spanish name equal to its GeoNames name, else its first Spanish
  name, else its GeoNames name, cut to the shortest known name it starts with («Toluca» for «Toluca de Lerdo»,
  «Puebla» for «Puebla de Zaragoza»): listings often print the short form, and a short name also matches the long one
  as whole words. Historic names, names of a past period and colloquial names are not used, except a colloquial name
  that is also the preferred one («León», «Cali»).
- A country keeps its preferred Spanish name, else its first Spanish name, else its English name.
- Columns: `name`, ISO 3166-1 `country`, `latitude` and `longitude` (three decimals, about 110 m), `population`,
  `seat` (1 for `PPLC` and `PPLA`). Lines starting with `#` are comments.

The data contains information from GeoNames (https://www.geonames.org), licensed under CC BY 4.0; the selection and
reduction above are the changes. `packaging/licenses/NOTICE.md` carries the attribution in the installed application.

## Trust boundary

Positions come from the operating system through `yt-app` and enter as `Coordinates`, which reject anything outside
the Earth's range. The table is compiled into the binary; a row that does not parse, names an unknown country or does
not fit a `RegionPreferences` is left out, and a test checks that no row is.

## Performance

The table is parsed once, on the first lookup (about 10 ms in release builds, 1.8 MB of rows); each lookup then takes
about 1 ms. `yt-app` calls `zone_at` on a blocking thread, at most a few times per session.

## Limits

- Places of fewer than 15,000 people are not in the table; a village leads to the nearest town of that size or to
  the city of its area.
- Near a border the nearest place may be across it, and the zone then follows that country.
- Twin cities of similar size can resolve to the other one, for example Minneapolis to Saint Paul (the state seat)
  and Viña del Mar to Valparaíso.
- Some official names are longer than the one listings print and have no shorter Spanish form in GeoNames
  («Santiago de Querétaro»); the user can type the city instead.

## Verification

`cargo test -p yt-geo` checks the coordinate bounds, great-circle distances, that every row of the embedded table is
used, the zones of suburbs of Ciudad de México, Monterrey, Guadalajara, Nueva York, Madrid, Barcelona, Bogotá,
Medellín, Lima, Santiago, Buenos Aires and Londres, cities on both sides of the Mexican border, and open sea.
`cargo test -p xtask` checks how Spanish names are chosen.
